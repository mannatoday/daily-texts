關於 Moravian Daily Texts

起源

《Die Losungen》（摩拉維亞每日箴言，英文 Moravian Daily Texts）自 1731 年印行以來每年出版，是持續最久的每日靈修讀本之一。

1722 年，波希米亞與摩拉維亞的信仰難民來到親岑多夫伯爵（Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf，1700–1760）的領地，建立赫恩胡特（Herrnhut，「主的守望」）。1727 年 8 月 13 日，弟兄合一會在那裡經歷屬靈更新。1728 年 5 月 3 日晚間聚會，親岑多夫把一句聖經交給會眾，作為第二天整天的口令。他稱這句話為 Losung，意思是守望的口令。此後每天有人把這句話送到村裡三十二戶，並一起談論。

親岑多夫為一整年編了 365 則守望經文，1731 年在赫恩胡特印出第一本。封面引用耶利米哀歌：主的慈愛每早晨都是新的。早期版本就把經文和一節聖詩放在一起：經文是神對人說的話，詩節是會眾的回應。1732 年弟兄會開始海外宣教，箴言也跟著傳到各地。今天以六十多種語言出版。每天的聖經章節仍在德國赫恩胡特選定，再分送到各地的語言版本。

Losung 與 Lehrtext

現在每一天有兩節，選法不同。

舊約的 Losung（守望經文，Watchword）是抽籤得出的。自 1788 年起，從一批適合日常的舊約經文中抽籤；到 1812 年確定，守望經文一律出自舊約。它多半是應許、鼓勵、勸勉或安慰，陪伴人過這一天。親岑多夫說，這些箴言是救主與教會持續的對話。

新約的 Lehrtext（教訓經文）不是抽籤，而是按當天的守望經文挑選。這個做法從親岑多夫為小組編的經文匯編長出來。1760 年他過世後，同工把守望經文和教訓經文分開保存，1762 年在巴爾比印成四卷。1801 年的說明是：守望經文是應許、鼓勵、勸戒或安慰；教訓經文則點出一項已經啟示的道理。十九世紀末起，兩節在主題上彼此呼應。

每日內容

每一天通常包含：

  • 一段舊約守望經文（Watchword）
  • 一段新約經文
  • 一段簡短禱告
  • 讀經進度（詩篇、舊約、新約）

舊約與新約並陳，提醒我們整本聖經彼此呼應：神的應許與成全、律法與福音，都在每日閱讀中相遇。

今日禱文

頁面上的「今日禱文」就是這一段簡短禱告。它回應當天的舊約與新約，把人帶到向神說話。篇幅只取一節聖詩，或一小段禱文，句末署名作者與作品。

這段禱文按當天兩節挑選，並加入華人文化裡的聖詩、禮文與靈修文字，以及歷代中文聖詩與公禱文。

讀經進度

頁面上稱為「經文選讀」。詩篇、舊約、新約各自按順序往下讀，與當天兩節守望經文各走各的。

平日（星期一至六）三條一起推進。長詩篇拆成幾天；舊約大約一天一到兩章，新約大約半章到一章，切在段落結束的地方。

照這進度，詩篇大約一年讀完一遍；詩篇以外的舊約與新約大約兩年讀完。一年大約讀完整本聖經的一半。跟著每天的選讀走，大約兩年可以讀完聖經一遍。

About Moravian Daily Texts

Beginnings

Die Losungen (Moravian Daily Texts) has been published every year since 1731. It is one of the longest-running daily devotionals.

In 1722, refugees from Bohemia and Moravia settled on the estate of Count Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf (1700–1760) and founded Herrnhut, “the Lord’s watch.” On 13 August 1727 the Moravian Church experienced a spiritual renewal there. On the evening of 3 May 1728, Zinzendorf gave the congregation a Bible verse to carry through the next day. He called it a Losung, a watchword. The verse was then carried to the village’s thirty-two households, and they talked about it together.

Zinzendorf prepared 365 watchwords for a full year, and the first book was printed at Herrnhut in 1731. The cover quoted Lamentations: the steadfast love of the Lord is new every morning. Early editions already set a Bible text beside a hymn verse. The text is God speaking; the verse is the congregation’s reply. In 1732 the Moravians began mission overseas, and the texts traveled with them. They are now published in more than sixty languages. The verses for each day are still chosen in Herrnhut, Germany, and then sent to the different language editions.

Watchword and doctrinal text

Each day now has two texts, chosen in different ways.

The Old Testament Losung (Watchword) is drawn by lot. From 1788 it was drawn from Old Testament verses suited to daily life; by 1812 the watchword was always from the Old Testament. It is usually a promise, an encouragement, an admonition, or a comfort for the day. Zinzendorf said these texts were an ongoing conversation between the Savior and the church.

The New Testament Lehrtext (Doctrinal Text) is not drawn by lot. It is chosen to go with that day’s watchword. The practice grew out of the collections Zinzendorf prepared for small groups. After his death in 1760, his colleagues kept the two texts separately, and in 1762 they were printed in four volumes at Barby. An explanation from 1801 said that the watchword is a promise, encouragement, warning, or comfort, and the doctrinal text names a truth already revealed. Since the late nineteenth century the two texts have answered each other.

What each day contains

A day usually includes:

  • an Old Testament watchword
  • a New Testament text
  • a short prayer
  • a reading plan: Psalms, Old Testament, and New Testament

The Old and New Testaments are set side by side, so promise and fulfillment, law and gospel, meet in the day’s reading.

The prayer

In Chinese, “今日禱文” is that short prayer. It answers the day’s two texts and turns the reader toward speaking to God. It is one hymn stanza or a short prayer, with the author and the work named at the end.

The prayer is chosen for those two texts and draws on Chinese hymns, liturgy, and devotional writing, together with historic Chinese hymnals and books of common prayer.

Readings

On the page these are the Readings. The Psalms, the Old Testament, and the New Testament each continue in their own order, apart from the two daily texts.

From Monday through Saturday the three tracks move forward together. A long psalm may take several days. The Old Testament is about one or two chapters a day, and the New Testament about half a chapter to a chapter, broken where a passage ends.

At this pace the Psalms are read through about once a year, and the rest of the Bible about once in two years. One year covers about half of the Bible. Following the daily readings, the Bible is read through in about two years.

This site

The Chinese edition follows the official daily verses. It is not a translation. Scripture is quoted from Chinese versions licensed for worldwide use, and the version can be changed at the top of the page. The prayer is chosen for the day’s two texts and draws on Chinese hymns and prayers. The site is for personal and family devotion and is not an official publication.
